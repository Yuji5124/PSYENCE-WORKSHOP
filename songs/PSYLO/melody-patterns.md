# Melody patterns — three eight-bar chorus trials

The browser preview's triangular tone is a pitch guide, not a vocal performance. MIDI numbers below use scientific pitch notation with C4 = MIDI 60. The drafts stay around B minor / D major color, but the bass and guitar can alter that impression. They are sketches to sing against, not locked toplines.

| Pattern | Tempo | Phrase behavior | PSYLO pronunciation | First decision to test |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| A — LONG O · selected direction | 160 BPM (150–165) | Short lyric phrases build through bars 1–6; the hook rises across bars 7–8 | **サ** on bar 7 beat 3, **イ** on beat 4; highest note F#5 begins **ロー** on bar 8 beat 1 and holds about two beats | Compare guitar continuing under **ロー** with a two-beat guitar gap |
| B — DOUBLE HOOK | 136 BPM (125–145) | Backbeat and offbeat hats make a gently bouncing repeated hook | Two short **サイロ** calls, then two **PSYLO!** calls | Does repeating the word make it instantly memorable? |
| C — SWITCH & SHOUT | 148 BPM (135–155) | Electronic guide leads the first bars; sampled guitar enters at bar 3 | **PSY** stretches, **LO!** snaps high and short | Does the texture change make the last shout feel earned? |

## A hook backing comparison

The app keeps tempo, notes, lyric timing, and levels fixed between these two A variants:

- **RUN:** the guitar eighth-note figure continues under the held **ロー**.
- **DROP:** the guitar leaves the first two beats of bar 8 clear, then returns for the rest of the bar.

The guide remains a synthetic pitch cue. A singer or licensed singing synthesizer must still test whether the long **o** is comfortable and memorable at this pitch.

These pitches are new working material. The references in `research-notes.md` are listening prompts for broad construction principles, not melody sources.
