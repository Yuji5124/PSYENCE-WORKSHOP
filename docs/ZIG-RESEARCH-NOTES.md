# ZIG Research Notes

## Purpose

This notebook studies hide's publicly documented creative thinking as context for new work by ZIG. It is not a plan to reproduce hide, X Japan, or any existing recording. New experiments must use original melodies, riffs, sounds, and arrangements.

The notebook is designed to change as sources are checked. At this stage it contains research questions and a recording method, not verified historical claims.

## Evidence labels

- **FACT** — a claim that can be checked directly against a reliable source. Record the exact source and the relevant page, date, or timestamp.
- **QUOTE** — exact words from hide or another named speaker. Preserve the original wording, identify the speaker, and add a translation separately when needed.
- **INTERPRETATION** — our reading of a fact or quote. Keep it visibly separate from what the source itself says.
- **OPEN** — a lead or question that still needs a source. Do not use it as a factual claim.

Prefer first-party material, contemporaneous interviews, published books and liner notes, and reputable archival or music-industry sources. When sources disagree, keep both accounts and document the disagreement rather than silently choosing one.

## Research questions

1. How did hide describe the relationship between experimentation and accessibility?
2. What did he say about contrast, surprise, humor, speed, or play in creative work?
3. Which production or collaboration choices are documented by first-hand sources?
4. How did the tools and music culture of the time shape those choices?
5. Which ideas can become present-day creative questions without borrowing identifiable musical material?

These are questions, not claims about hide's views.

## Turning research into experiments

For every interpretation, write a question rather than a style recipe. Make one small experiment that tests that question, then record what was surprising, what felt compelling, and what should change. Do not treat a broad idea such as contrast or play as evidence that hide used a particular technique unless a source documents it.
