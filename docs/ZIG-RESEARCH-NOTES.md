# ZIG Research Notes

## Purpose

This notebook studies hide's publicly documented creative thinking as context for new work by ZIG. It is not a plan to reproduce hide, X Japan, or any existing recording. New experiments must use original melodies, riffs, sounds, and arrangements.

The notebook is designed to change as sources are checked. At this stage it contains research questions and a recording method, not verified historical claims.

## Evidence labels

- **FACT** — a claim that can be checked directly against a reliable source. Record the exact source and the relevant page, date, or timestamp.
- **QUOTE** — exact words from hide or another named speaker. Preserve the original wording, identify the speaker, and add a translation separately when needed.
- **INTERPRETATION** — our reading of a fact or quote. Keep it visibly separate from what the source itself says.
- **OPEN** — a lead or question that still needs a source. Do not use it as a factual claim.

Prefer first-party material, contemporaneous interviews, published books and liner notes, and reputable archival or music-industry sources. When sources disagree, keep both accounts and document the disagreement rather than silently choosing one.

## Research questions

1. How did hide describe the relationship between experimentation and accessibility?
2. What did he say about contrast, surprise, humor, speed, or play in creative work?
3. Which production or collaboration choices are documented by first-hand sources?
4. How did the tools and music culture of the time shape those choices?
5. Which ideas can become present-day creative questions without borrowing identifiable musical material?

These are questions, not claims about hide's views.

## Turning research into experiments

For every interpretation, write a question rather than a style recipe. Make one small experiment that tests that question, then record what was surprising, what felt compelling, and what should change. Do not treat a broad idea such as contrast or play as evidence that hide used a particular technique unless a source documents it.

### RN-001 — Unexpected playback becoming a musical starting point
- **Evidence label:** FACT (attributed recollection)
- **Claim:** In the BARKS interview, I.N.A. recounts that the song 'POSE' emerged after data from a different song played unexpectedly and the resulting sound was found compelling.
- **Speaker / author:** I.N.A., interviewed by BARKS.
- **Source ID:** S001
- **Location in source:** Interview passage discussing 'POSE' and unintended playback.
- **Context and limits:** A collaborator's later recollection, not contemporary documentation or an exact hide quotation.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high that I.N.A. reported this; lower for the precise historical mechanics without corroboration.
- **INTERPRETATION:** The creative decision may be not to discard a surprising accident immediately.
- **Creative question for ZIG:** Can a deliberate input mismatch create a musically appealing eight-second surprise?
- **Possible experiment:** Swap one drum or pitch-pattern track between two independently seeded original sequences, audition blind, then save only the intriguing outcomes.
- **Originality check:** Do not reconstruct 'POSE' or borrow its riff, melodic contour, samples, arrangement or sound recording.

### RN-002 — An idea may exceed the tool
- **Evidence label:** FACT (attributed recollection)
- **Claim:** I.N.A. contrasted earlier production, when creative ideas often exceeded available computer capability and required workarounds, with modern workflows that may start from the capabilities of software.
- **Speaker / author:** I.N.A.
- **Source ID:** S002
- **Location in source:** Interview page 2, passage comparing ideas and computer capabilities.
- **Context and limits:** This is I.N.A.'s judgment in a later interview. It is **not** a direct statement by hide.
- **Confidence:** High for the existence of the interview statement; this is a perspective rather than an objective law of creation.
- **INTERPRETATION:** Design the desired sonic surprise first, then choose or build the mechanism.
- **Creative question for ZIG:** Which desirable sound is presently impossible with the Phase 0 sequencer, and what is the smallest modification to test it?
- **Possible experiment:** Write a one-sentence target sound before opening the app; implement only one new manipulation if required.
- **Originality check:** Build new material rather than imitating historical recordings.

### RN-003 — Hybrid rhythmic construction
- **Evidence label:** FACT (attributed recollection)
- **Claim:** In the official anniversary interview, I.N.A. describes combining drum performances by multiple players and machines and associates this approach with what hide called cyborg rock.
- **Speaker / author:** I.N.A.
- **Source ID:** S003
- **Location in source:** Discussion of drum editing and work on '子 ギャル'.
- **Context and limits:** The reported production practice is a recollection; statements about later production are not automatically evidence of how any particular earlier recording was made.
- **Confidence:** High for the interview statement; recording-specific production assertions require separate checking.
- **INTERPRETATION:** Interesting rhythm can be created from the friction between independent human-like and machine-like timings.
- **Creative question for ZIG:** What happens when separate groove layers preserve different timing personalities?
- **Possible experiment:** Combine two original step-sequenced drum grooves, each with distinct swing/microtiming, and preserve the tension rather than quantizing everything.
- **Originality check:** No hide samples, original drum recordings, or recognizable song arrangements.

- **Speaker / author:** Reporter writing the 2020 retrospective.
